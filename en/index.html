01

The decision it serves

Context

A Chinese manufacturer cannot bid in a Saudi public tender in its own name. The award is signed by a Saudi company that holds the product registration and import license, and carries the after-sales obligation. That company decides what is sold, at what price, and to which hospital.

Every Chinese manufacturer selling here works through a local supplier — usually exactly one. So the market splits in two: manufacturers already carried by someone, and manufacturers still open. You can only win the second kind. Nobody tells you which is which.

If you walk the floor at WHX Dubai in January, or review your lines in any other month, the question is the same. That is the whole point of counting award lines: a manufacturer with registrations and no award line has a product that works in this market and no one selling it. That is not a quality judgement. It is a statement about who is currently moving the goods.

03

Scope and limits

Limits

NUPCO publishes no prices. We cannot tell you what anyone charged or was paid. An award line is a signature, not a shipment: it says nothing about volume, stock, or whether the goods were ever delivered. 45.8% of award lines could not be assigned to a product category, so any category-level figure covers only the lines we could classify.

A large share of the lines we attribute to a Chinese manufacturer is in fact third-party product described as “compatible with” a Chinese instrument — not made by that manufacturer. We separate those out rather than count them as the manufacturer's record. You can see this in the notices yourself. It moves the numbers substantially, so we report both figures side by side.

Private hospitals, group purchasing organizations and markets outside Saudi Arabia are not covered. Public tender awards are one channel; they are not the whole market.
